RESEARCH REPORT: Impact of Remote Work on Employee Productivity

ABSTRACT
This study examines productivity changes among 500 knowledge workers 
who transitioned to remote work between 2023-2024. Data was collected 
via surveys and productivity tracking software over 12 months.

METHODOLOGY
Participants were drawn from technology, finance, and consulting sectors. 
Productivity was measured using task completion rates, output quality 
scores, and self-reported focus time. A control group of 150 in-office 
workers was used for comparison.

KEY FINDINGS
Remote workers showed a 13% increase in task completion rates compared 
to the control group. However, collaborative task quality scores decreased 
by 8%, particularly for tasks requiring cross-team coordination. Self-reported 
focus time increased by 22% among remote workers, attributed to fewer 
interruptions.

Workers with dedicated home office spaces showed significantly better 
outcomes (p<0.01) than those working from shared or temporary spaces.

CONCLUSIONS
Remote work increases individual task productivity but may negatively 
impact collaborative work quality. Organizations should consider hybrid 
models that preserve focus time benefits while creating structured 
collaboration opportunities.

RECOMMENDATIONS
1. Implement core collaboration hours (e.g., 10am-2pm) for synchronous work
2. Provide stipends for home office setup
3. Use asynchronous tools for non-urgent collaboration
4. Conduct quarterly team cohesion assessments
